# Research notes: «Неизвестный художник: Адольф Гитлер»

## Editorial position

The project should present the subject as a historical art-and-provenance case study, not as a celebration of Adolf Hitler. The interface will foreground uncertainty of attribution, the failed artistic career, the later political crimes, and the risks of turning contested objects into memorabilia.

## Confirmed points

The supplied Russian Wikipedia article describes Hitler's activity as an artist before his political career, with work in Vienna and Munich, especially postcards and watercolors. It also includes a catalogue-style image section with works such as a self-portrait and a watercolor of the Old Residence courtyard in Munich.

History.com reports that Hitler was rejected twice by the Vienna Academy of Fine Arts, later made small oil and watercolor paintings of buildings and landmarks, and that several hundred works attributed to him are known to survive. It also notes that authentication is difficult because the style is difficult to distinguish from many amateur works of the period.

The BBC reported in 2019 that German police seized three watercolors attributed to Hitler over suspected forgery. The ABC reported that five works attributed to him failed to sell at auction amid similar concerns, and that many works are held by institutions or private collections rather than public exhibitions.

## Asset rule

Do not copy images from Wikipedia or news pages into the new repository unless the individual file's license and attribution are verified. Prefer Wikimedia Commons assets with explicit licenses, or use text metadata and linked references when image rights are unclear.
